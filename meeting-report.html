Civic Notices Platform

Strategic Meeting Report & Action Plan
Meeting with Nick Semper | November 2025

Executive Summary

What I'm Building: A digital platform that modernizes how legal notices (licensing applications, planning notices, road closures) are published and managed in the UK.

Why It Matters: The government is abolishing the 300-year-old requirement to publish notices in newspapers. Councils currently spend £150,000+ per year on newspaper advertising. My platform reduces this to essentially zero while improving public engagement and democratic transparency.

This Meeting: Nick Semper (experienced licensing consultant) is joining to help position the platform. He's sponsoring a major industry conference this week (2,500+ attendees) where he'll showcase my work to licensing officers, councillors, and legal professionals across the UK.

The Opportunity

Market Size

2,000-3,000 notices daily 50,000 active notices 350+ local authorities

Revenue Potential: Even capturing 10% of the market represents approximately £3.6 million annual revenue from applicant fees alone (before council subscriptions).

What Makes This Different

The existing solution (Public Notice Portal) just aggregates newspaper notices. When newspapers are no longer required, it becomes useless. My platform provides:

  • Direct Response Capability: Public can submit objections/support directly through the platform
  • Council Management Portal: Departments can build templates, manage representations, track analytics - all in one place
  • Complete Workflow: Not just publication, but end-to-end case management
  • Analytics They Don't Have: Councils literally don't know how many notices they process annually - I give them this data

Strategic Decisions Made

Pricing Strategy - Major Pivot

Decision: Offer the platform to councils FOR FREE initially (or first 10 adopters get lifetime free access).

Why:

  • Adoption is make-or-break - if councils don't use it, the platform fails
  • Creates a "no-brainer" decision for councils (save £150k/year with zero cost)
  • Revenue comes from applicants (£49.99 per notice vs. current £400-600)
  • After proving value, introduce minimal fees (e.g., £5/month per department)

Technical Improvements Needed

Legal Compliance Features (High Priority)

When someone objects to a licensing application, UK law requires them to specify which of the four licensing objectives their objection relates to:

  1. Prevention of crime and disorder
  2. Public nuisance
  3. Public safety
  4. Protection of children from harm (under 18)

Action: Add checkboxes for these objectives in the objection form. Support submissions (non-objections) just need a text box.

Key Features to Build

1. Council Analytics Dashboard

Councils don't know how many notices they process. When I asked the Head of Planning for North Yorkshire Council, he said: "I have no idea. I literally couldn't give you a number."

Opportunity: Provide them with data they've never had - notice volume, representation statistics, geographic trends, etc.

2. Blue Notice Photo Tracking

Nick's company posts physical "blue notices" on buildings (legal requirement). They photograph them as proof of compliance. This entire workflow should integrate into my platform - upload photos, timestamp them, generate proof-of-posting certificates.

3. Representation Management System

Councils need to review all objections/support submissions. Give them a proper inbox: filter by type, mark as reviewed, add internal notes, generate hearing reports.

4. Data Population from Council Websites

Legally, I can scrape council websites (not the Public Notice Portal). Estimated 50,000 active notices to import. This gives immediate value - the platform is useful from day one rather than starting empty.

5. Expand Beyond Licensing

Planning applications, road closures, traffic regulation orders, environmental permits - all use the same publication model. Each expansion multiplies the addressable market.

Competitive Landscape

Current Solutions

Public Notice Portal: Just aggregates newspaper notices. Will become obsolete when newspaper requirement ends.

Individual Council Websites: "Absolutely crap" according to Nick. "Clanky and unfriendly." No standardization, poor user experience, no representation submission.

My Platform: Complete workflow solution with council management, public engagement, and compliance built-in. First-mover advantage in the post-newspaper era.

Risk Mitigation

Technical Risks

  • Scalability: 50,000+ notices to import, 1,500 new per day. Need to upgrade infrastructure (manageable - TypeScript/React handles this, Supabase scales).
  • OCR Accuracy: Extracting data from PDFs needs high accuracy. Building confidence scoring and manual review for low-confidence extractions.
  • Bugs During Demos: Current template builder has some issues. Nick advised offering free access initially so councils are forgiving of early glitches ("Show me how much money you've lost from that glitch. None? Then it's fine.").

Market Risks

  • Slow Council Adoption: Councils are notoriously slow to change. Mitigation: Free access removes financial barrier, Nick's network provides warm introductions.
  • Legislative Changes: Government might mandate a specific solution. Mitigation: Position as the obvious solution through early adoption and case studies.
  • Competition: Someone else could build this. Mitigation: First-mover advantage, conference exposure this week, Nick says the people he's talking to "aren't the type to go build it themselves."

Personal Reflections

What I'm Learning

This meeting reinforced several important lessons:

  • Adoption > Features: Getting councils to actually use the platform is more important than building every possible feature. Free access removes the biggest barrier.
  • Positioning Matters: Framing this as "improving democracy" and "saving taxpayer money" resonates more than technical features.
  • Network Effects: Nick's relationships open doors I couldn't access alone. His endorsement carries weight.
  • Market Timing: Legislative change creates urgency. The 300-year-old newspaper requirement is ending - I need to be ready when it does.
  • Forgiveness in Exchange for Free: If I give councils free access, they'll tolerate early bugs. That removes the pressure to be perfect before launching.
