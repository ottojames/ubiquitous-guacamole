# Compulsory Purchase Orders (CPOs) Research

## Executive Summary

CPOs represent a **£2-4 million annual market** with 200-400 orders per year. Very expensive per order (£5,000-£20,000 in publication costs). Civic Notices can offer **30-50% cost savings** through platform aggregation.

## Legal Framework

- **Compulsory Purchase Act 1965**: Conditions for purchase
- **Acquisition of Land Act 1981**: Procedural requirements
- **Town and Country Planning Act 1990**: Section 226 powers
- **Land Compensation Acts 1961 & 1973**: Compensation rules
- **Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023**: Recent updates

## Who Can Use CPOs?

### Local Authorities
- County councils, district/borough councils
- National park authorities
- Mayoral development corporations

### Central Government
- Government ministers
- Homes England
- Health service bodies

### Statutory Undertakers
- Utility companies (electricity, gas, water, telecoms)
- **Network Rail** (railway expansion, electrification)
- Airport operators, port authorities

### Special Purpose Bodies
- Urban development corporations
- New town development corporations

## Publication Requirements

### Newspaper Publication (TWO SUCCESSIVE WEEKS)
- One or more local newspapers
- Must include:
  - Statement that CPO is being submitted
  - Minimum 21-day objection period
  - How to make objections
  - Where documents can be inspected

### Site Notices
- On or near affected land
- "Conspicuous" and accessible
- Throughout objection period

### Personal Notices
- Served on **all qualifying persons**:
  - Owners
  - Occupiers (including all tenants)
  - Those with rights (easements, covenants)
- Must include Statement of Reasons

### Digital Publication
- Website relating to scheme
- Throughout objection and confirmation process

### Post-Confirmation
- Confirmation notices in newspapers (within 6 weeks)
- Site notices explaining High Court challenge rights
- Personal notices to all qualifying persons

## Types of CPOs

1. **Housing/Regeneration**: Slum clearance, estate regeneration, affordable housing
2. **Highway**: Road building, bypasses, improvements
3. **Planning/Development**: Town centre redevelopment, economic regeneration
4. **Utilities**: Power, water, gas, telecoms infrastructure
5. **Transport**: Railway, airports, ports
6. **Conservation**: Listed buildings in disrepair

## Market Size

### Volume
- **200-400 CPOs annually** across UK
- Major transport projects: 20-30/year
- Housing/regeneration: 100-150/year
- Utilities: 50-80/year

### Geographic Distribution
- London & South East: 35-40%
- Major cities: 25-30%
- Transport corridors: 20-25%

### Costs Per CPO
- Newspaper advertising: £2,000-£8,000
  - Small town: £1,500-£3,000
  - City centre: £4,000-£8,000
  - Major infrastructure: £8,000-£15,000
- Site notices: £200-£500
- Personal service: £500-£2,000
- Legal/admin: £1,000-£3,000
- **Total per CPO: £5,000-£20,000**

### Market Value
- 300 CPOs × £8,000 average = **£2.4M annual market**
- Growth drivers: Infrastructure investment, housing targets, net zero transition

## Current Market Problems

1. **Fragmented supply chain**: Multiple newspaper relationships
2. **Pricing opacity**: No standardized pricing
3. **Administrative burden**: Manual processes
4. **Limited digital integration**: Print-focused approach

## Example Notice Text

```
ACQUISITION OF LAND ACT 1981
TOWN AND COUNTRY PLANNING ACT 1990 - SECTION 226

[AUTHORITY NAME]
NOTICE OF MAKING OF COMPULSORY PURCHASE ORDER

The [Authority] (Land at [Location]) Compulsory Purchase Order 2026

Notice is hereby given that on [Date], the [Authority] made the above-named Order under section 226 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.

The Order relates to land at [Location] as shown on the map available for inspection at [Address] between [Times] until [Date].

Any objection must be made in writing to [Secretary of State Address] before [Deadline - minimum 21 days].

[Signed]
[Authority]
```
