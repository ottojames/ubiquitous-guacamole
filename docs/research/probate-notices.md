# Probate Notices Research: Trustee Act Section 27

*Research compiled: January 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Section 27 of the Trustee Act 1925 provides a mechanism for executors and administrators to protect themselves from personal liability to unknown creditors and beneficiaries when distributing an estate. The traditional approach involves placing statutory advertisements in The London Gazette and local newspapers, but insurance-based alternatives are increasingly disrupting this market.

**Key Market Opportunity**: With ~570,000 deaths annually in England & Wales, and The Gazette/newspaper notice costs of £350-400+ per estate, this represents a potential market of **£150-200m+ annually** for statutory advertising services.

---

## Legal Framework

### Trustee Act 1925, Section 27

**Purpose**: Allows trustees/personal representatives to protect themselves from liability against claims from creditors or beneficiaries they had no notice of at the time of distribution.

**Requirements**:
1. Place a notice in **The Gazette** (London, Edinburgh, or Belfast edition depending on jurisdiction)
2. Place a notice in a **local newspaper** circulating in the district where any land/property is situated
3. Allow a minimum of **2 months** for claims to be submitted
4. After this period, the executor can distribute without personal liability to unknown claimants

**Key Point**: This is *recommended* but **not legally mandatory**. However, failure to advertise means personal representatives remain personally liable for unknown debts discovered after distribution.

### Equivalent Legislation
- **Scotland**: Confirmation of Executors Act 1823
- **Northern Ireland**: Trustee Act 1958

## Current Market: How Executors Advertise

### 1. The London Gazette (Primary Channel)

The Gazette is the **official public record** and the dominant player in this market.

**Website**: thegazette.co.uk

**Pricing (2026)**:
| Service | Cost (ex VAT) |
|---------|---------------|
| Deceased estates notice (webform/template) | £96.55 |
| Deceased estates notice (other submission) | £131.70 |
| PO Box forwarding service | £66.45 - £87.55 |
| Local newspaper placement | £240.00 (flat rate) |
| PDF voucher copy | £8.10 - £11.15 |

**Total typical cost**: £336.55 - £371.70 + VAT (~£400-450 inc VAT)

**Process**:
1. Obtain grant of probate, letters of administration, or death certificate
2. Create account on The Gazette website
3. Complete online form with deceased details
4. Upload required documentation
5. Select additional services (PO Box, newspaper placement)
6. Pay and submit
7. Wait minimum 2 months + 1 day before distributing estate

**The Gazette's Combined Service**:
- Place Gazette notice AND local newspaper notice in one step
- Flat £240 fee for any newspaper placement
- They handle finding/placing with appropriate local paper
- Provides PDF proof of publication

### 2. Local Newspapers

Required when the deceased owned property/land - must advertise in paper "circulating in the district" where property is located.

**Traditional Process**:
- Identify appropriate local newspaper
- Contact classified ads department
- Submit notice text (typically 50-100 words)
- Pay per column inch or word count
- Costs vary significantly by publication

**Cost Range**: £100-300+ per paper (highly variable)

**The Gazette's Solution**: £240 flat rate handles all local placement

### 3. Solicitors/Probate Professionals

Many solicitors handle notice placement as part of full probate services:
- Include in "Probate Complete Service" packages
- Markup on Gazette/newspaper costs
- Add administrative fees
- Total probate service costs: £2,000-10,000+ depending on estate complexity

## Insurance Alternatives: The Emerging Disruptor

### What is Section 27 Insurance?

An indemnity policy that **replaces the need for statutory advertisements** entirely.

**How it works**:
- Personal representatives buy insurance instead of placing notices
- Policy covers claims from unknown creditors discovered later
- Protects both executors AND beneficiaries
- No 2-month waiting period required
- Estate can be distributed faster

### Cost Comparison (£250,000 estate)

| Method | Cost | Time Required |
|--------|------|---------------|
| Traditional S.27 notices | ~£390 | 2+ months wait |
| S.27 Insurance (via solicitor) | £119 | Immediate |
| S.27 Insurance (DIY with support) | £202 | Immediate |
| S.27 Insurance (fully DIY) | £238 | Immediate |

### Advantages of Insurance

1. **Speed**: No mandatory 2-month waiting period
2. **Cost**: Often cheaper than traditional advertising
3. **Coverage**: Protects beneficiaries too (notices don't)
4. **Lifetime cover**: No excess, permanent protection
5. **Privacy**: No public disclosure of estate details

### Limitations of Traditional Notices

1. **No beneficiary protection**: Only protects personal representatives
2. **Mandatory delay**: 2+ month waiting period
3. **Public disclosure**: Notice permanently in public domain
4. **Cost**: Often more expensive than insurance
5. **Outdated**: "Antiquated" newspaper requirement

## Pain Points & Opportunities

### Current Market Problems

1. **Fragmented process**: Multiple steps across different platforms
2. **Confusing requirements**: Many executors unsure what's legally required
3. **Outdated newspaper requirement**: Digital world, analog solution
4. **Price opacity**: Local newspaper costs highly variable
5. **2-month delay**: Frustrates beneficiaries, creates pressure on executors
6. **Technical barriers**: The Gazette platform not user-friendly for lay executors

### Opportunities for Digital Disruption

1. **One-stop platform**: Combine Gazette + newspaper + insurance options
2. **Price transparency**: Clear, flat-rate pricing
3. **Process simplification**: Guided workflow for lay executors
4. **Digital-first local advertising**: Online local news sites vs print
5. **Insurance integration**: Offer both traditional and insurance options
6. **Solicitor white-label**: API for probate software integration

## Competitive Landscape

### The Gazette's Position

**Strengths**:
- Official status - mandatory for full S.27 protection
- Government-backed credibility
- Combined newspaper service eliminates friction
- Data/API services for professionals

**Weaknesses**:
- Government/legacy platform - slow innovation
- Complex pricing structure
- Not designed for lay users
- No insurance alternative offered

### Estatesearch's Position

**Strengths**:
- One-stop shop for probate searches
- Professional-focused
- Bundled services

**Weaknesses**:
- Price opacity (POA)
- Not consumer-facing

### Insurance Providers

**Strengths**:
- Faster, often cheaper
- Better protection coverage
- Modern digital experience

**Weaknesses**:
- Not a complete replacement (doesn't fulfill statutory requirement technically)
- Less awareness among solicitors/executors
- Regulatory perception issues

## Strategic Recommendations

### For a New Entrant (e.g., Civic Notices)

1. **Position as modern alternative**: "The faster, clearer way to protect executors"

2. **Offer hybrid model**:
   - Traditional S.27 notices (Gazette + newspaper)
   - S.27 insurance option
   - Let executors choose based on circumstances

3. **Target audiences**:
   - DIY executors (underserved, confused)
   - Small/medium probate solicitors (seeking efficiency)
   - Will writers adding probate services

4. **Pricing strategy**:
   - Transparent flat-rate pricing
   - Bundle Gazette + newspaper at competitive rate
   - Insurance alternative for speed-focused clients

5. **Differentiation**:
   - Modern UX vs Gazette's dated interface
   - Plain English guidance
   - Combined search + notice + insurance platform
