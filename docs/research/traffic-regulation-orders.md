# Traffic Regulation Orders (TROs) Research

## Executive Summary

Traffic Regulation Orders represent a **£50-65 million annual market** with strong regulatory tailwinds. Government is actively reviewing publication requirements (Jan 2024 DfT consultation), signaling clear appetite for digital transformation.

## Legal Framework

**Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984** grants local traffic authorities power to make orders for:
- Parking restrictions and controlled zones
- Speed limits and traffic calming
- Weight restrictions
- One-way systems and turning restrictions
- Bus lanes and cycle lanes
- Pedestrian zones and school streets

### Authorities
- County councils: 353
- Unitary authorities: 27
- London boroughs: 32 + City of London
- Transport for London (strategic routes)

## Types of TROs

### Permanent TROs
Long-term regulations. 6-12 months to implement. Full consultation required.

### Temporary TROs (TTROs)
Short-term (up to 18 months). Used for:
- Roadworks and utility works
- Special events
- Emergency repairs

### Experimental TROs (ETROs)
Trials for up to 18 months. Testing new traffic schemes before permanent implementation.

### Emergency TROs
Immediate safety concerns. Can implement within hours.

## Publication Requirements

### Newspaper Advertisement (21 days)
- **Primary cost driver**
- Must publish at least 21 days before order takes effect
- Include full restriction details
- Specify inspection locations
- Provide objection period details

### On-Street Notices
- Posted at or near restriction location
- Visible to road users and residents
- Protected from weather during notice period

### London Gazette
- Required for trunk roads / strategic importance
- Additional £200-400 cost
- Extended timelines

### Public Inspection
- Full documentation at council offices
- Online publication increasingly common
- Free copies on request

## Market Size

### Volume
- **15,000-20,000 TROs** made annually across UK
- Average 40-50 per authority per year
- London boroughs: 80-120 annually
- County councils: 60-100 annually

### Costs
- Average newspaper ad: £800-1,200 per TRO
- Complex schemes: £1,500-3,000
- London/metro areas: £1,000-2,000
- Rural areas: £400-800

### Total Market
- Newspaper advertising: **£50 million**
- Administrative costs: £15-20 million
- **Total: £65-70 million annually**

### Geographic Distribution
- London: 3,000-4,000 TROs annually (25% of national)
- Top 50 authorities: ~60% of total volume
- Metropolitan areas: highest growth

## Current Players

### Council In-House
Most councils manage TROs internally:
- Resource-intensive (multiple departments)
- Manual processes prone to delays
- No economies of scale

### TSO (The Stationery Office)
- London Gazette publications
- High costs, limited digital innovation
- Not well-adapted to local authority needs

### Local Newspapers
- Declining circulation
- Reduced publication frequency
- High costs relative to reach
- Uncertain long-term viability

## Regulatory Tailwinds

**January 2024 DfT Consultation:**
- Proceeding with amendments to Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984
- Targeting newspaper advertising requirement
- Alternative digital publication methods
- Greater flexibility in publication
- Reduced costs for authorities

## Civic Notices Opportunity

### Competitive Advantages
- Digital-first, searchable platform
- Integrated mapping (precise restriction locations)
- Automated alerts by location
- Significant cost savings vs newspaper
- Full accessibility compliance
- Real-time analytics on engagement
