# Planning Notices Market Research (UK)

*Last updated: January 2026*

## Executive Summary

Planning notices represent a significant segment of the UK statutory advertising market, with local authorities spending an estimated **£28-50 million annually** on newspaper publication requirements. The market is under pressure from digital transformation advocates but remains protected by legislation requiring newspaper publication for certain application types.

---

## 1. How Planning Notices Work

### The Planning Process
When a planning application is submitted to a Local Planning Authority (LPA), the authority must publicise the application to allow public consultation before making a decision. This publicity takes multiple forms:

1. **Website publication** - mandatory for all applications
2. **Site notices** - posted on or near the land
3. **Neighbour notification letters** - to adjoining owners/occupiers
4. **Newspaper advertisements** - required for specific application types

### Consultation Period
- Standard consultation period: **21 days** (14 days for newspaper notices)
- Public Service Infrastructure Development: **18 days**
- EIA applications with environmental statement: **30 days**

---

## 2. Statutory Requirements

### Primary Legislation
The requirements for publicising planning applications are set out in:

- **Town and Country Planning Act 1990** (Section 65)
- **Town and Country Planning (Development Management Procedure) (England) Order 2015** (Articles 13 & 15)
- **Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Regulations 1990**

### Article 15 - Key Publicity Requirements

| Application Type | Website | Site Notice | Newspaper |
|-----------------|---------|-------------|-----------||
| **EIA applications** (with environmental statement) | ✓ | ✓ (30 days) | **✓ Required** |
| **Major development** (10+ dwellings, 1000m²+ commercial) | ✓ | ✓ or neighbour letters | **✓ Required** |
| **Departure from Development Plan** | ✓ | ✓ (21 days) | **✓ Required** |
| **Affecting a public right of way** | ✓ | ✓ (21 days) | **✓ Required** |
| **Listed building consent** | ✓ | ✓ | **✓ Required** |
| **Conservation area development** | ✓ | ✓ | **✓ Required** |
| **Minor/householder applications** | ✓ | ✓ or neighbour letters | Not required |

### What Must Be Published
The newspaper notice must contain:
- Address or location of the proposed development
- Description of the proposed development
- Date by which representations must be made (not less than 14 days)
- Where and when the application may be inspected
- How representations may be made

### Mineral Applications (Article 13)
Additional requirements apply for underground mineral operations:
- Must publish in newspaper circulating in the locality
- Site display in every parish within the application area
- Notice must remain for minimum 7 days within 21 days before application

## 3. Who Publishes Them?

### Local Planning Authorities
There are approximately **333 local planning authorities** in England alone, each responsible for:
- Selecting appropriate local newspapers
- Placing advertisements
- Ensuring compliance with statutory timeframes

### Newspaper Publishers
The statutory advertising market is dominated by a small number of large publishers:

**Major Regional Publishers:**
- **Reach PLC** - Manchester Evening News, Liverpool Echo, Birmingham Mail, etc.
- **Newsquest** (Gannett) - Multiple regional titles
- **JPI Media** - Yorkshire Post, Scotsman, etc.
- **Archant** - Eastern region titles
- **Midland News Association** - Express & Star

Most local newspapers are owned by approximately **6 major media groups**, giving them significant pricing power.

### Key Statistics
- Nearly **900 local and regional news titles** are NMA members
- These reach **42 million people** monthly across print and digital
- Print circulations have declined dramatically (e.g., Manchester Evening News print circulation now under 10,000)

## 4. Market Size

### Total Spending Estimates

| Source | Estimate | Year |
|--------|----------|------|
| LGA Survey | **£28 million+** | 2023 |
| Parliamentary Evidence | **£40-50 million** | 2015 |
| Guardian/Killian Pretty Review | **£15 million** (planning only) | 2009 |

### Regional Examples
- **Lincolnshire councils**: £3.1 million over 5 years (~£620k/year)
- **Manchester City Council**: £228,761.93 paid to Reach Publishing Services (2024/25)
- **Hampshire councils**: Significant spend prompting collaborative review

### Market Composition
The £28-50m total includes:
- Planning application notices
- Licensing notices (pubs, premises)
- Traffic regulation orders
- Road closure notices
- Environmental permits
- Public path orders
- Compulsory purchase orders

**Planning-specific notices** likely represent approximately **£15-25 million** of the total.

## 5. Reform Pressure & Policy Debate

### Arguments for Reform

**Local Government Association Position:**
- Describes current requirements as a "taxpayer subsidy of the commercial newspaper industry"
- Conducted 2023 survey to build evidence for reform
- Advocates for digital-first alternatives

**Key Critics' Points:**
- Declining newspaper readerships mean few actually see notices
- High costs set by monopoly local publishers
- Better alternatives exist (targeted digital, social media, direct mail)
- Independent digital news sites cannot benefit from statutory revenue

### Arguments Against Reform

**News Media Association & Publishers:**
- Notices are "integral to the public's right to know"
- Support democratic transparency
- Serve digitally excluded populations
- Provide vital revenue supporting local journalism

### Government Response
- **2009**: Killian Pretty Review recommended removing newspaper requirements; rejected by government
- **2014-15**: Eric Pickles funded £950,000 for 24 pilot programmes to test alternatives
- **2023**: DCMS Committee recommended review; no significant change implemented
- **2025**: Chancellor Rachel Reeves signalled potential reforms for licensing notices
- **Planning and Infrastructure Bill**: Proposes changes to DCO consultation (may reduce some notices)

### Cairncross Review (2019)
Noted that "it is not clear that statutory notices are still an effective" means of public communication, but no reforms enacted.

## 6. Digital Solutions & Competitors

### Government/Public Sector

**Planning Portal** (planningportal.co.uk)
- Established 2002; privatised 2015
- Joint venture: UK Government & TerraQuest Solutions
- Primary platform for submitting planning applications electronically
- Provides guidance and interactive tools
- Does NOT replace newspaper notice requirements

**Idox Public Access**
- Leading provider of government planning software
- Used by majority of UK LPAs
- Provides online searchable planning registers
- Citizen-facing portal for viewing applications

**The Gazette** (thegazette.co.uk)
- Official journal of record
- Publishes statutory and non-statutory notices
- Permanent public record
- Used for insolvency, company, royal notices (limited planning relevance)

### Industry-Led Digital Solutions

**Public Notice Portal** (publicnoticeportal.uk)
- Operated by **News Media Association**
- Collaboration with **Google News Initiative**
- Aggregates notices published in NMA member newspapers
- Searchable by postcode and notice type
- Free public access
- Categories: Planning, Licensing, Traffic, Environment

**Key Limitation:** Only aggregates notices from print newspapers; does not replace statutory requirement.

### Private Sector Software

**Idox Group** (idoxgroup.com)
- Market leader in UK planning software
- Powers most council planning portals
- Uniform case management system
- Public Access citizen interface

**Digital Land** (digital-land.github.io)
- DLUHC initiative
- Building open data infrastructure for planning
- Working toward single register of planning applications

### Independent News Sites (Emerging)
- **The Mill** (Greater Manchester) - Example of independent local journalism
- Cannot currently receive statutory notice revenue (not "newspapers")
- Argue they could reach more people than declining print titles
- Potential beneficiaries of reform

## 7. Opportunities & Market Gaps

### Current Pain Points
1. **Fragmented market** - 333+ LPAs, hundreds of newspapers
2. **High costs** - Limited competition, monopoly local publishers
3. **Declining effectiveness** - Print readership collapsed
4. **No standardisation** - Each LPA negotiates separately
5. **Legacy technology** - Many still manual/fax-based processes

### Potential Opportunities

**Aggregation & Efficiency:**
- Centralised booking platform for multi-LPA notices
- Negotiated group rates with publishers
- Automated compliance checking

**Digital Hybrid Solutions:**
- Combined print + digital packages
- Geo-targeted digital advertising
- Social media distribution
- SMS/email notification services

**Data & Intelligence:**
- Planning notice tracking and alerts
- Property development intelligence
- Market analytics for developers/investors

**If Regulation Changes:**
- Pure digital notification platforms
- Alternative "newspaper" qualification pathways
- Mobile-first public notice apps

## 9. Summary: Key Takeaways

1. **Market Size**: £28-50m total statutory notices; £15-25m planning-specific
2. **Mandatory Newspaper**: Still required for major, EIA, listed buildings, conservation area applications
3. **Pressure for Change**: Strong LGA/digital advocates vs. newspaper industry resistance
4. **No Imminent Reform**: Despite reviews, fundamental requirements unchanged since 2015
5. **Digital Supplements**: Portal aggregation and online planning registers exist but do not replace print
6. **Fragmented Market**: 333+ LPAs, 6 major publishers, limited competition
7. **Opportunity Space**: Efficiency gains through aggregation, hybrid digital/print, compliance automation
