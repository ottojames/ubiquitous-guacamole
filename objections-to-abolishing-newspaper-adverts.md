# Objections to Abolishing Newspaper Adverts

## Context

Gareth Hughes (Licensing & Planning Barrister, Keystone Law) posted on LinkedIn arguing for the abolition of compulsory newspaper advertising for licensing notices under the Licensing Act 2003 reform.

### His Main Arguments:
- Newspaper ads cost £300-£600+ and "almost nobody reads" them
- Blue notices on premises do the "genuine democratic work"
- Council portals and Public Notice Portal provide "searchable, cost-free access"
- Print advertising is an "expensive exercise" and "vestigial levy" on small operators
- Evidence from 2001 showed under 1% of people found out about applications through newspapers

### His Proposed Solution:
✅ Keep premises blue notices
✅ Keep digital publication (council website + Public Notice Portal)
❌ Remove compulsory print classifieds

---

## Critical Flaw in the Argument

### Public Notice Portal Research

**Contact Information:**
- Email: support@publicnoticeportal.uk
- No phone support available
- Website: https://publicnoticeportal.uk

**Key Finding: They Are An Aggregator, Not A Publisher**

### Exact Quotes from Public Notice Portal Website:

> **"Public Notice Portal receives all of its Public Notice data from local news publishers across the UK."**

> **"Currently there is no way for businesses or public users to publish notices directly to Public Notice Portal."**

> **"If you would like to publish a Public Notice, we recommend contacting either your local news publisher, or your local authority, depending on the requirements of your notice."**

> **"The portal carries statutory public notices published in local newspapers and is the fastest and most effective way of finding out what is happening in YOUR neighbourhood."**

> **"The Public Notice Portal is owned and operated by the News Media Association, the voice of UK national, regional, and local newspapers in all their print and digital forms."**

### The Counterargument

**The barrister's argument has the causation backwards:**

- Public Notice Portal is **downstream** of newspaper publication, not a replacement for it
- The portal **depends on** newspapers publishing notices first
- Abolishing newspaper publication would **eliminate the portal's data source**
- The portal is an **aggregator of already-published notices**, not a primary publication platform

So while the portal is excellent for searchability, it's actually downstream of newspaper publication rather than independent. Worth factoring into the reform conversation - we'll need an alternative publicly accessible primary publication mechanism for the UK if print goes?"

- Positions as "something to factor in" not "you're wrong"

My one concern is infrastructure readiness: are councils currently equipped with consistent, reliable publication systems that can serve as the statutory record if newspapers are removed? There's a gap between recognising the reform is right and ensuring the replacement mechanisms are in place and working before we flip the switch. We need that bridge built first."

**Strategic Objectives:**
- Support move to digital (aligns with our business)

- Position as practical/timing concern, not opposition to digital

## Key Takeaways

1. **Public Notice Portal cannot replace newspapers** - it depends on them as its data source
2. **Infrastructure gap exists** - no consistent primary digital publication mechanism currently in place
3. **Reform direction may be right** - but sequencing matters (build replacement before removing existing system)
4. **Our positioning**: Digital is better, but needs purpose-built primary publication infrastructure (which we're building)

## Message for Stakeholders

This means:
- You can't abolish the source and expect the aggregator to survive
- We need alternative primary publication infrastructure in place BEFORE removing newspapers
- The reform is sensible directionally, but implementation requires new digital infrastructure
- That infrastructure needs to be consistent, reliable, and serve as the statutory record

This is exactly what we're building - a proper digital primary publication platform that can serve as the replacement mechanism, not just an aggregator of existing publications."
